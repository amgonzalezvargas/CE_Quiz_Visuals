Case Study & Quiz: Three Failure Trajectories (Customer Service Agent)

0/30 points

An online footwear retailer uses an AI customer service agent to handle post-purchase requests. A customer purchased trail running shoes in Size 8 for $120 (Order #4412, delivered Oct 1st) that are too tight. They reach out to the assistant asking to exchange them for a Size 8.5.

From the initial order lookup, the interaction branches into three distinct failure trajectories: Trajectory 1: Information Quality (Context Poisoning & Context Clash), Trajectory 2: Action Selection (Context Confusion), and Trajectory 3: Volume & Noise (Context Distraction & Context Rot).

Question 1:

10 points

In Trajectory 1, when the agent checks the exchange rules for the customer's order, an outdated 14-day policy is retrieved from the document store and treated as valid, followed shortly by a conflicting 60-day policy. What technical term describes this dual failure mode where incorrect information enters the working context and later conflicts with an updated record?

Question 2:

10 points

In Trajectory 2, the customer tells the agent to cancel the order during an exchange request, and the model mistakenly executes a cancellation tool instead of an exchange tool because their tool descriptions overlap. Which mechanism is primarily responsible for defining what a tool does and guiding the model toward the correct selection?

Question 3:

10 points

In Trajectory 3, an off-topic web search dumps raw HTML into the context window, causing distraction, while a long-running chat history eventually leads the model to forget early details like the order number. Which core phenomenon explains why models struggle to utilize all parts of a large context reliably over lengthy sessions?
